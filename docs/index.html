A study of satellite scene classification

Beyond the frame

Can a computer name a place more accurately when it also sees what surrounds it?

This tool lets you find out. Give it a satellite image and it reads the scene three times: a tight crop of the spot itself, a wider look at its neighbourhood, and the whole landscape. It names the scene type, then shows how much weight each view received.

    The study
    
    
    What we asked, and what we found
    
    
    Four short chapters. Start anywhere.
    
  
    

  
    
    
    
    
    
  
    

  
  
    
    
    
      
    
        Question 1
        
    Does a wider view help?
    
        
    Start with the 256 m square at the center, then add a 768 m view and the full 1.28 km scene. All three go through the same frozen network, so any change in accuracy comes from what the model was shown.
    
      
    
      
    
        Question 2
        
    Does it help most in the desert?
    
        
    Bare ground, sand and scrub look alike up close. Our guess was that the neighbourhood matters most for those scenes. We fixed which classes count as arid before looking at any result, and we report the gap with a bootstrap 95% interval.
    
        
    Arid classes
      
      
      
      
      
        Question 3
        
      Does it travel to the UAE?
      
        
      We tried the model on Sentinel-2 images of the UAE at 10 m per pixel, ten times coarser than the training imagery. The labels were checked by eye and the model was not retrained. Classes with fewer than 5 accepted samples are skipped. Treat the outcome as a sanity check, since the set is small.
      
      
      
    
      
  
      

  
  
      
    
      
      
      
        
      One place, three views
      
        
      Each scene is 1280 × 1280 pixels, about 1.28 km across at 1 m per pixel. We cut three centered crops from it and shrink each to 224 × 224 pixels. The network always receives the same number of pixels, and each view covers a different amount of ground.
      
        
      
        
      What the model receives 
      
        
      
      
      

      
      
        
      Three looks, one answer
      
        
      One frozen MobileNetV2 reads each view. A small gated head then learns how much each view should count and blends them into a ranked list of the 24 scene types. During training of the -VD versions, views are switched off at random, so the head copes when one is missing. Press the button to see that happen.

      Limits
      
      What this study cannot tell you
      
      
        
        
      • MEET has no coordinates. We cannot check whether training, validation and test scenes overlap on the ground. Near-duplicates were screened by exact file hash and by feature similarity to the closest training image.
        • 
        
      • The UAE set is small. Up to 25 candidates per class, and only classes with at least 5 accepted samples are scored. Dry field, Hills and Military training ground were not sampled at all.
        • 
        
      • The UAE imagery is a different sensor. Sentinel-2 at 10 m per pixel (winter 2025-26), ten times coarser than MEET. Five small-object classes cannot be sampled at that resolution. Nothing was retrained or tuned on UAE.
        • 
        
      • B1 against B2 says little about context. The custom CNN trains end to end on the center view and has a different architecture from the frozen-MobileNetV2 head.
        • 
        
      • Gates are weights. A larger gate means the head leaned on that view's features. It does not show that the view caused the answer.
        • 
        
      • Grad-CAM is a visual aid. It shows where a class score is sensitive. It does not prove what the model understands.
        • 
      
      
    
      
  
      

  
  
      
    
      
      
      
        
      How the study was run
      
        
      Open any block for the details. The aim is that someone else could repeat the work.
      
        
      Data and sampling
          
        
            
      • 24 classes drawn only from the official MEET split lists, never reshuffled. Per class and split: min(N, available), with N = 200 train, 50 validation and 100 test, seed 42.
        • 
            
      • Views are nested centered crops of the 1280 px scene, each resized to 224 × 224 (bicubic in the export pipeline): center box (512, 512, 768, 768), surround (256, 256, 1024, 1024), global the full image.
        • 
          
      
        
      
        
      Training and tuning
          
        
            
      • Heads train on cached features from a frozen ImageNet MobileNetV2. Seeds 42, 7 and 123 for every head. Seed 42 for the data and for B1.
        • 
            
      • Tuning: keras-tuner RandomSearch, 10 trials each for B3-VD and B4-VD, scored by macro-F1 on the whole validation set. The test set and UAE never influenced tuning.
        • 
          
      
        
      
        
      Statistics
          
        
            
      • Exact McNemar test on paired test predictions, per seed, with effect sizes.
        • 
            
      • Bootstrap 95% CI on per-class context gain: test images resampled within each class, paired across models.
        • 
          
      
        
      
        
      Reproducing it, and what differs in the browser
          
        
            
      • Pinned environment in requirements.txt and requirements.lock.txt. Run order in run_all.sh.
        • 
            
      • This site resizes images with the browser canvas, which does not give bit-identical pixels to the export pipeline's bicubic resize. Only the head is parity-tested.
